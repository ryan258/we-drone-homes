We Drone Homes copy and launch kit

Use this guide to write, publish, or hand off a piece of We Drone Homes. The project is a fictional agency and interactive comedy portfolio. “Flight Plan for a House” is the campaign idea. It takes the ordinary job of photographing a home and gives it the treatment of a flight operation. The house gets the beauty shot. The agency gets the joke.

Voice and editorial rules

Write in the tone of a competent property photographer who has filled out one form too many. Give the image or task a precise description, then let one ordinary detail deflate the ceremony. Let the joke land on the fictional agency, the branding, or the furniture. Keep headlines in sentence case and use all caps for short labels such as FLIGHT REPORT / 01. Use one memorable line per card or screen. Use the word fictional where a visitor might otherwise assume a real service, price, listing, testimonial, or action.

Situation Use Keep out of the lead
Launch or introduction “Killer views. Unfortunate name.” with the visible clarifier and a property image A violent incident, weapon imagery, or ambiguous “book air support” copy on its own
Property caption “A golden-hour fly-through. The golden retriever had notes.” Unsupported performance or safety claims, exact addresses in off-site art, real MLS identifiers
Testimonial Visible “Fictional client” credit with an obviously invented character Real client names, review-platform badges, claims that a rating is independently verified
In-world fleet or field notes Camera terms, mock operational formality, weather and household details How-to instructions for reckless flying, certification claims, government emblems, real affiliation
Package and quote “Fictional packages” and “Imaginary total” next to the amount Payment language that implies the visitor owes money or has secured a booking
Interaction and error Clear action and result, with a short joke only after the task is clear A jokey label that obscures what a button, error, or permission prompt does

Put a meaningful verb and object in control labels. A play or download symbol can reinforce the text. Keep the visible words at the start of the accessible name so the visitor can say what they see. Describe what an image contributes and include its essential on-image text when that text appears nowhere else. Mark purely decorative glyphs, brackets, and paths as decorative. Explain a result with text as well as color. Offer the written route and manual steps alongside any flight animation, and keep sound optional.

Press note and creator pitch

Press note headline
We Drone Homes gives four imaginary houses the full flight briefing

Press note body
We Drone Homes is an interactive comedy project about a fictional real estate drone agency with a regrettable name and a fondness for flight plans. Visitors can tour four imagined properties, play through illustrated flight paths, compare fictional photo packages, and make a mock flight brief. The cast includes a suburban golden retriever with notes and a chandelier that has declined comment.

The project borrows the format of a polished property portfolio, then applies the formality of a flight operation to everyday features such as a foyer, a skylight, and a dog door. “Killer views. Unfortunate name.” is the agency’s line, and also its most concise explanation.

Press assets
Brand lockup, landscape campaign card, portrait chandelier card, listing still, short project boilerplate, and image credits. Add the owner-approved project URL and contact before sharing the note with a recipient.

Short pitch
I made We Drone Homes, a fictional real estate drone agency. It looks like a property portfolio until every listing gets a flight report. The McMansion Monolith is a quick place to start. There’s a twilight still, a simulated flight, and a chandelier that declined comment. I can supply artwork and credits if you’d like to feature it.

A small launch sequence

  1. Review the homepage, listing page, and mock-booking entry as three separate ways into the project. Confirm that the headline, visible fiction label, destination, and credited image agree. Check the exported card at thumbnail size and with the caption hidden.
  2. Lead with the ranch card on an owner-approved project page or social profile. Use the portfolio as the destination so readers can choose a property. Keep the creator pitch and press note ready if the owner wants to share them.
  3. Follow with the chandelier card and the direct McMansion link. Use the listing still in an editorial or portfolio post that explains how a single property became an interactive flight report. Reuse the existing report if there is interest in a teaser.

Keep the project’s no-analytics default. Ask a small group of fresh readers what they think the site offers, then give them the portfolio link and ask them to find a flight report. A proposed launch gate is that all five people in a first pass can identify the offer as fiction from a detached card or entry page, and at least four can open a property report and describe what its flight control does without a prompt. Review one keyboard-only path separately. Ask participants which image or detail they would share and why. This is a practical review with a small group. Record actual observations, fix any recurring confusion, and keep campaign reach and audience fit as open questions until observed.
