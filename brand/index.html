02 / Brand platform

The house is the hero.

Position We Drone Homes as a playable real estate photography parody. The listings look familiar; the agency’s treatment of a living room as a flight operation is the joke.

What it is

Fictional agency. Real experience.

Explore invented properties, meet the pilots, scrub a simulated flight route, and put together an imaginary booking. The site is the work; there is no photography service behind it.

What visitors get

A familiar format with a second layer.

The listing makes sense at a glance. A mission report turns it into a short piece of interactive comedy. One room, one memorable detail, one clear next step.

Who it is for

Property obsessives first.

Lead with the house for people who share unusual listings. Show the interaction to creative and web peers. Give real estate and FPV insiders an extra layer in the details.

Brand promise
A great view of an ordinary home, delivered with an extraordinary amount of procedure.
Positioning
We Drone Homes is an interactive property-listing parody for people who enjoy beautiful houses and take pleasure in how seriously we talk about them.
Character
Observant, precise, dry. The voice of a capable camera crew reviewing an entirely avoidable naming decision.
Message order
We Drone Homes → “A fictional real estate drone agency” → “Killer views. Unfortunate name.” → “Explore the fictional portfolio.” In art, the tagline can be largest; keep the descriptor legible in the same frame.

Headlines / Arial Bold

Killer views.

Sentence case and tight tracking. Scale from 48–99 px for a hero and 35–59 px for a main section. Keep each line short and the joke on one line where it fits.

Reading / Arial Regular

The whole property. The perfect light.

Start body copy at 16 px with 1.6 line height. Use links with visible labels and enough context to make sense when read alone.

Page and composition

  • Use an 8 px spacing rhythm for new assets. Use generous outside margins, close label-to-title spacing, and one image anchor per frame.
  • Pair a one- or two-column editorial lead with cards that can collapse into a single column. Keep critical details out of edge crops.
  • Use straight edges and ruled dividers. Use a plain paper or ink block when text needs a reliable background.
  • In print, export from vector masters, allow 3 mm bleed when requested, and proof colors on actual stock; hex values are screen masters.

Interaction and access

  • Use ink on paper, white, or signal; use paper or signal on ink. Signal on paper is a decoration. The line color is a divider, with a darker border for controls that need a boundary.
  • Keep action labels literal, with a visible focus indicator. Aim for a 44 × 44 CSS px hit area. Confirm status with words, including when a stage or filter changes.
  • Show the flight path as a labeled sequence as well as a drawing. Make motion optional and sound opt-in; provide pause, stop, and a text alternative.
  • Describe meaningful photos in alt text. Hide a glyph when it repeats visible text; label it if it carries the action on its own.

Web component recipes

Property card
Image → fictional-property label → property name → one room detail → “View flight report.” Start with an exterior when the property is unfamiliar. At narrow widths, keep the caption below the image so it remains readable.
Package card
Package name → fictional price → what the package contains → “Choose this mock package.” Give the featured tier a visible “Featured” label as well as a different surface. Mark the price as fictional at the comparison or quote level.
Route panel
Caption the diagram “Illustrative simulation.” Pair numbered waypoints with a text sequence and expose “Play simulated flight,” “Pause,” and “Reset flight.” When paused, retain the current step and its name.
Forms and feedback
Put the mock label before inputs. In empty, error, and completed states, say what happened and give one next step: “Choose a fictional property” or “Mock flight brief ready. Save flight brief.”

05 / Art direction

Three views
of the same brand.

Use these boards to brief an illustrator, choose a crop, or create the next property feature. The three boards are applications of one visual system.

Photography brief: make one plausible architectural choice and one comic choice. Use stable horizons, natural window light, and recognizable homes. Reserve crop room on the subject side as well as for text. Keep overlays away from property details and reserve simulation labels for any illustration that looks like telemetry. Keep weapons, explosions, surveillance cues, fake damage, and identifiable addresses out of campaign art.

06 / Verbal identity

A clean shot.
A straight face.

Describe what the visitor can see or do, then add one small absurdity. The copy can be playful; the instructions should still work when someone skips the joke.

Anchor line

Killer views. Unfortunate name.

Use it once per campaign surface. Pair with “A fictional real estate drone agency” on standalone materials.

Property copy

The chandelier declined comment.

Pick a specific room or object. The chandelier, third kitchen, or uncooperative windmill earns the gag.

Action copy

Play simulated flight.

Tell people what the button does. Use “Create mock booking” and “Download fictional brief” when a label could otherwise sound like a real service.

Outside the fiction
Lead with the plain descriptor in credits, bios, captions, metadata, and media pitches. An image repost or screenshot should still make sense by itself.
Inside the fiction
Use lightly formal labels like “Location scout,” “Flight report,” and “Field notes.” Keep invented testimonials, prices, awards, endorsements, or turnaround claims clearly identified when used.
Writing boundaries
Put the joke on the agency’s inflated language and minor household objects. Avoid real client details, implied endorsement or certification, actual emergency language in calls to action, and unsafe flight advice.

08 / Positioning & rollout

Make the premise
travel well.

Share an image people want to look at, then reward the click with a flight report. Treat the first rollout as a small test of comprehension and interest.

Strength / weakness

Strong visual premise and working interactions. The believable package and booking language can become ambiguous in isolation.

Opportunity / threat

A listing image is an easy entry point for property humor. Cropped assets can lose context, and one joke will wear out if every post retells it.

  1. Introduce: use the landscape share card and link to the fictional portfolio.
  2. Show the play: follow with one room detail and one simulated report, paired with a still and captions.
  3. Show the craft: share how the flight diagram or dispatch works as a small creative case study.
